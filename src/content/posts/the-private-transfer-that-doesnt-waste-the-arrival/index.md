## Pay for certainty, not theater

The best transfer is not the biggest car. It is the one that knows the flight, the luggage count, the hotel entrance, and whether you need water, a pharmacy stop, or silence. That is what separates a useful arrival from a decorative one.

## The email to send

One message, sent the week before, covers almost everything a driver needs:

- Flight number and the names on the booking.
- **Exact luggage count**, including anything oversized — this is what decides the vehicle.
- Child seats, if needed, with ages rather than sizes.
- One sentence about the first evening: dinner booked, pharmacy stop, or nothing at all.
- Whether you want the scenic road, or the fastest route and no commentary.

Luxury service improves when the brief is direct. It belongs in the same note as [the concierge email to send before you land](/post/the-concierge-email-to-send-before-you-land/), because both are answering the same question: what should already be handled when the doors open.

> A car cannot make an arrival luxurious. It can stop the arrival from spending the first evening.
>
> <cite>Mara Chen, on what the hour from the airport is actually buying</cite>

## Where it pays back

Transfers are most valuable after overnight flights, at resorts far from the airport, and in cities where taxis add friction at the exact moment you are least patient — the same calculation that decides whether [a first-class lounge](/post/when-the-first-class-lounge-is-worth-the-layover/) is worth the layover. They are least useful in compact rail cities where the station is already the best entrance. Spend for the hour that protects the first night.
