## Rain reveals the resort

A resort designed only for sun collapses quickly: wet paths, nowhere comfortable to read, restaurants that feel empty at lunch, rooms too small to inhabit for more than sleep. Rain shows whether the property is a place or just a beach with keys.

## Look for depth

The resilient resort has covered walks, a proper library or lounge, a spa with capacity, a bar that works before dinner, and rooms with real seating. It has staff who can move a snorkeling day without drama and kitchens that do not treat lunch as an afterthought.

## Book the better weather plan

Before committing to an island stay, ask what guests do on rainy days. A confident answer tells you the resort has imagined the full trip. A list of excursions tells you it expects the weather to be someone else's problem.
